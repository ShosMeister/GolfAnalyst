Golf Analyst Home - Info

This is the Home screen. It shows where you stand overall and has the buttons that take you to everything else. Every number on it is worked out by the program from the rounds you have saved. None of it is typed in.

# The Handicap section (left)

Handicap Index: Your current USGA Handicap Index. It follows the rules of the World Handicap System: your best 8 rounds out of your last 20, each one adjusted for the difficulty of the course and tees you played. With fewer than 20 rounds on record, fewer of your best rounds are used. You need at least 54 holes played before you have an index at all: three rounds of 18, or a mix of 18-hole and nine-hole rounds that adds up to 54 holes. Nine-hole rounds count toward your index as long as the nine you played has a rating. It shows n/a until then.

Hole limit: For the handicap, no single hole counts for more than a net double bogey. That is par plus 2, plus any handicap strokes you get on that hole, so on a hole where you get a stroke it is par plus 3. Until you have a Handicap Index it is par plus 5. The program applies the limit itself, so one disaster hole cannot wreck your index. Your scores and statistics still show what you really wrote down.

Low Index: The lowest index you had during the last 365 days. The rules use it to stop your index from climbing too quickly. It only exists once you have 20 rounds on record.

Total Rounds, 18-Hole Rounds and 9-Hole Rounds: How many rounds you have saved. A nine-hole round counts as a round of its own. It is not added to another nine to make an 18. Historical rounds are counted here only when Include historical rounds (see below) is ticked.

Rounds Used: How many of the rounds on record count toward your index, out of how many there are (up to 20), for example 8 of 20.

# Historical rounds

A historical round is an old round kept to look back at, such as the rounds brought over from the Excel version. It never counts toward your Handicap Index. Whether it counts in your statistics, the Stats Board and Best Round is up to you: tick or untick Include historical rounds under Golfer's Stats. It starts ticked, because your old rounds are still true facts about your game. Untick it to see how you play today. When you step through your rounds, a historical round says "historical" in the 18-Hole Diff box, and the bar at the bottom says so too.

# The Golfer's Stats section (right)

These are the numbers that help you find the weak spot in your game. They use every round you have saved (old historical rounds too, if Include historical rounds is ticked) and are not course specific. The Stats Board shows them course by course. All of the percentages are rounded to one decimal place.

Name: Your name, kept in settings.txt in your data folder.

Average (18 holes) and Average (9 holes): A straight average of your 18-hole rounds and of your nine-hole rounds. They are kept apart because a nine-hole round cannot be compared with an 18-hole one.

Personal Best: Your best 18-hole round and your best nine-hole round, in that order.

Fairways Hit: The percentage of fairways you hit from the tee. Only par 4s and par 5s are counted, since on a par 3 you are aiming at the green.

Sand Saves: The percentage of times you were in the sand and still made par or better. It does not matter whether it was a greenside or a fairway bunker.

Greens In Reg: The percentage of holes where you reached the green in regulation, which means you had a putt for birdie or better. The strokes you took before your first putt (your score minus your putts) must be no more than par minus 2. That is why a putt only counts when you are on the green. A chip-in for a birdie on a par 4 is not a green in regulation. A hole in one is.

Two-Putt: The percentage of holes where you took two putts or fewer. Whatever is left over was three putts or more.

Include historical rounds: Decides whether old (historical) rounds count in the numbers in this section, in the round counts on the left, on the Stats Board and in Best Round. Ticked, they do. Unticked, only your recent rounds do. It never changes your Handicap Index, Low Index or Rounds Used, which never include historical rounds. The program remembers your choice.

# The buttons in the middle

The buttons on the left are for putting information in. The buttons on the right are for looking at it.

On the left: New Course adds a course and its tees, or fixes a course you have already saved. New Round is the screen for entering a round. Edit Round is for correcting or deleting a round you have saved, Import is for loading many rounds or courses at once, and Clear empties your rounds, or everything, so you can start fresh. It asks twice and keeps a copy of your files first.

On the right: Rounds is the screen for looking at the rounds you have saved. Stats Board, Analyzer and Best Round show your statistics in more detail, and Print prints what you are looking at.

Import, Analyzer and Print are not built yet. They are in place and will be added.

# Across the top and bottom

The round house button on the left takes you back here from any other screen. The i button, this one, explains what is on the screen you are looking at. The ? button explains how to use it. The bar along the bottom shows messages. If a problem was found in your data files it tells you how many, and clicking it lists them.

The yellow Golf Analyst text at the bottom left of every screen is the version of the program. You can also hold the mouse button down on it to see the photo; the Help explains how.

# About Golf Analyst

Welcome to the Golf Analyst - a program written by Angelo Mileto.

The original version of this program was written in 1991 in HyperCard on the Mac, before the USGA came up with the Slope system for handicapping. It has been rewritten several times since, and this version runs on Windows, Mac and Linux.

# History: Mac HyperCard Version
Golf Analyst v2.10: Written by Angelo Mileto, September 1991 in HyperCard. Special thanks go out to Dave Speer and the gang at Computer Solutions in San Antonio, Texas, without whose help this stack would not have been possible.

Golf Analyst v2.11: Updated May 1992 to correct a minor bug in the par calculation.

Golf Analyst v2.12: Updated May 1992 to correct a bug I created while fixing the bug in the par calculation :)

Golf Analyst v2.20: Updated June 1992 to add in the USGA Slope Calculations.

Golf Analyst v2.30: Updated May 1994 to correct an error which caused the program to lock up if the scores were over 110.

Golf Analyst v2.50: Updated June 1997 to correct some minor typing errors and add the ability to track partial rounds. Also, the Best Score function was added.

The original background was designed by Dave Speer.

The Course Analyzer card was created by Malcolm S. Douglas.

# History: MS Excel Version
Golf Analyst v3.00: Completely rewritten in MS Excel to allow for use on both Mac and PC, August 2005. Background images were gathered from various sources and all copyright information remains intact. All functions are as the original program except all pre-slope calculations have been removed.

Golf Analyst v3.01: Fixed minor bugs and added the Import function and Handicap Report.

# History: Java Version
Golf Analyst v4.00: Rewritten in Java so that it runs on Windows, Mac and Linux without needing Excel. Your courses and rounds are kept in two plain text files that you can edit yourself. The Handicap Index now follows the current USGA rules (the World Handicap System). Nine-hole rounds are supported, which is handy for leagues like Spark Golf. Greens In Regulation and Sand Saves are now calculated instead of entered. The Last 5 and Partial Rounds are gone, since a nine-hole round is now a round of its own.

Golf Analyst v4.10: Added the New Course screen, so courses and up to four sets of tees at a time are typed in instead of edited into courses.tsv by hand. The window now tells you when you are looking at the sample data, and offers to make your own data folder before anything is saved there.

Golf Analyst v4.11: The New Course screen now looks like the round screen, with each set of tees in its own wooden frame. An arrow on the first tee copies its pars and handicap numbers into the empty cells of the other tees. Everything is checked as you type, and Save comes on only when the course is ready to save.

Golf Analyst v4.20: Added Edit Round, so a saved round can be fixed right on the screen instead of in the rounds file. Saved courses can be fixed the same way from New Course. Only what you changed is rewritten, and a copy of the file as it was is kept in rounds.tsv.bak or courses.tsv.bak. The Help and Info windows now show headings and bold labels.

Golf Analyst v4.21: The round screen now looks like the New Course screen, with the holes in neat grids inside wooden frames, a wooden column beside them for the rating and the round's figures, and the totals and buttons in a wooden frame underneath, all centred on the photo. Press and hold the yellow Golf Analyst text at the bottom left of any screen to see the whole photo.

Golf Analyst v4.22: The Help and Info windows now have to be closed before you can go back to the screen behind them, so the help you are reading always belongs to the screen you are on.

Golf Analyst v4.30: Old rounds can be kept as historical rounds. You can still look back at them in Rounds, but they are left out of your Handicap Index and your statistics, so those show how you play today.

Golf Analyst v4.40: Added the Stats Board, which shows your statistics course by course and tee by tee, with your nine-hole rounds kept apart. Click a heading to sort it, and the best figure in each column is marked in gold. Double-click a course to see its rounds.

Golf Analyst v4.41: Close on the Rounds screen goes back to the Stats Board when you came from there, instead of to the Home screen. Closing the window, pressing Home or pressing Cancel while you are part way through typing a new round or a course now asks "Discard your changes?" first.

Golf Analyst v4.50: Added the Best Round screen. For any course and set of tees it shows your lowest single round, or a composite of your best score on every hole, so you can see how close your best day is to your potential.

Golf Analyst v4.60: Added a Historical box on the round screens, so old rounds can be marked without editing a file, and an Include historical rounds box on the Home screen, so your statistics, the Stats Board and Best Round can count your old rounds or leave them out.

Golf Analyst v4.70: Added Delete Round and Clear, each with two confirmations. Every change to your files now keeps a dated copy in a backups folder, so nothing is ever overwritten.
