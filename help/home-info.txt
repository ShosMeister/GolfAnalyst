Golf Analyst Home - Info

This is the Home screen. It shows where you stand overall and has the buttons that take you to everything else. Every number on it is worked out by the program from the rounds you have saved. None of it is typed in.

# The Handicap section (left)

Handicap Index: Your current USGA Handicap Index. It follows the rules of the World Handicap System: your best 8 rounds out of your last 20, each one adjusted for the difficulty of the course and tees you played. With fewer than 20 rounds on record, fewer of your best rounds are used. You need at least 54 holes played before you have an index at all: three rounds of 18, or a mix of 18-hole and nine-hole rounds that adds up to 54 holes. Nine-hole rounds count toward your index as long as the nine you played has a rating. It shows n/a until then.

Hole limit: For the handicap, no single hole counts for more than a net double bogey. That is par plus 2, plus any handicap strokes you get on that hole, so on a hole where you get a stroke it is par plus 3. Until you have a Handicap Index it is par plus 5. The program applies the limit itself, so one disaster hole cannot wreck your index. Your scores and statistics still show what you really wrote down.

Low Index: The lowest index you had during the last 365 days. The rules use it to stop your index from climbing too quickly. It only exists once you have 20 rounds on record.

Total Rounds, 18-Hole Rounds and 9-Hole Rounds: How many rounds you have saved. A nine-hole round counts as a round of its own. It is not added to another nine to make an 18. Historical rounds follow the Include historical rounds setting described below.

Rounds Used: How many of the rounds on record count toward your index, out of how many there are (up to 20), for example 8 of 20.

# Historical rounds

A historical round is a saved round kept for reference, such as a round brought over from the Excel version. It never counts toward your Handicap Index. Whether it counts in your statistics, the Stats Board, Best Round and the Analyzer is up to you: check or uncheck Include historical rounds under Golfer's Stats. It starts checked, because your historical rounds are still true facts about your game. Uncheck it to see how you play without historical rounds included. When you step through your rounds, a historical round says "historical" in the 18-Hole Diff box, and the bar at the bottom says so too.

# The Golfer's Stats section (right)

These are the numbers that help you find the weak spot in your game. They use every round you have saved (historical rounds too, if Include historical rounds is checked) and are not course specific. The Stats Board shows them course by course. All of the percentages are rounded to one decimal place.

Name: Your name, kept in settings.txt in your data folder.

Average (18 holes) and Average (9 holes): A straight average of your 18-hole rounds and of your nine-hole rounds. They are kept apart because a nine-hole round cannot be compared with an 18-hole one.

Personal Best: Your best 18-hole round and your best nine-hole round, in that order.

Fairways Hit: The percentage of fairways you hit from the tee. Only par 4s and par 5s are counted, since on a par 3 you are aiming at the green.

Sand Saves: The percentage of times you were in the sand and still made par or better. It does not matter whether it was a greenside or a fairway bunker.

Greens In Reg: The percentage of holes where you reached the green in regulation, which means you had a putt for birdie or better. The strokes you took before your first putt (your score minus your putts) must be no more than par minus 2. Only putts made with your ball on the green are counted, so a stroke from the fringe counts as one of the strokes before your first putt. A chip-in for a birdie on a par 4 is not a green in regulation. A hole in one is.

Two-Putt: The percentage of holes where you took two putts or fewer. Whatever is left over was three putts or more.

Include historical rounds: Decides whether historical rounds count in the numbers in this section, in the round counts on the left, on the Stats Board, in Best Round and in the Analyzer. Checked, they do. Unchecked, only non-historical rounds are included. It never changes your Handicap Index, Low Index or Rounds Used, which never include historical rounds. The program remembers your choice.

# The buttons in the middle

The buttons on the left are for putting information in. The buttons on the right are for looking at it.

On the left: New Course adds a course and its tees, or fixes a course you have already saved. New Round is the screen for entering a round. Edit Round is for correcting or deleting a round you have saved. Import brings in rounds and courses from another copy of the program or from a spreadsheet. Export makes a copy of your courses and rounds that another copy of the program can Import. Clear empties your rounds, or everything, so you can start fresh. It asks twice and keeps a copy of your files first.

On the right: Rounds is the screen for looking at the rounds you have saved. Stats Board, Analyzer and Best Round show your statistics in more detail, and Print puts your handicap, your rounds, a scorecard and more on paper, in a PDF or in a spreadsheet file. Settings keeps your name, your choices about backups and which data folder the program uses.

Import: Adds rounds and courses from files laid out like rounds.tsv and courses.tsv. It only ever adds: a set of tees or a round you already have is skipped, and it never changes a tee or a round that is already in the program. It shows you what it will add before it writes anything, and keeps a copy of your files in the backups folder first.

Export: Makes a copy of your courses and rounds in a new folder of its own: everything, one course and its rounds, or your rounds from a date. It is laid out just like your own files, so another copy of Golf Analyst can bring it in with Import. Export only reads your files; it never changes them.

# Settings

The Settings button opens a window with these choices. Nothing changes until you press OK. They are kept in settings.txt in your data folder.

Your name: The name shown under Golfer's Stats.

Backups to keep: How many backups of each everyday kind the program keeps: 10, 20 (the usual), 50, 100 or All. The everyday kinds are edited rounds, deleted rounds and fixed courses; when there are more than this, the oldest go. Backups made by Import, Clear and Delete course are always kept.

Also copy every backup to: A second folder, such as a USB stick or a cloud folder, that gets a copy of every backup as it is made. It says not set when there is none. If that folder cannot be reached, your change is still saved and the bar along the bottom says the extra copy was not made.

Data folder: The folder that holds your courses.tsv, rounds.tsv and settings.txt. A different folder chosen here is used from the next time the program is opened, and the old one is left as it was.

Open backups folder: Shows the backups folder on your computer. Every backup is a folder of its own, named with the date, the time and what was done, for example 2026-11-01_143012_edit-round. Inside are copies of the files as they were, with their usual names.

On the sample data only your name is kept, and the data folder row offers to create your own data folder instead.

# Across the top and bottom

The round house button on the left takes you back here from any other screen. The i button, this one, explains what is on the screen you are looking at. The ? button explains how to use it. The bar along the bottom shows messages. If a problem was found in your data files it tells you how many, and clicking it lists them.

The yellow Golf Analyst text at the bottom left of every screen is the version of the program. You can also hold the mouse button down on it to see the photo; the Help explains how.

# About Golf Analyst

Welcome to the Golf Analyst - a program written by Angelo Mileto.

The original version of this program was written in 1991 in HyperCard on the Mac, before the USGA came up with the Slope system for handicapping. It has been rewritten several times since, and this version is built in Java, so it is meant to run on Windows, Mac and Linux.

ZIP code data from GeoNames (www.geonames.org), used under a Creative Commons Attribution license. The data is provided as is.

# History: MS Excel Version
Golf Analyst v3.00: Completely rewritten in MS Excel to allow for use on both Mac and PC, August 2005. Background images were gathered from various sources and all copyright information remains intact. All functions are as the original program except all pre-slope calculations have been removed.

Golf Analyst v3.02: Updated September 2006 to fix some minor bugs.

# History: Java Version
Golf Analyst v4.00: Updated November 2026 to rewrite the program in Java, so that it no longer needs Excel and is meant to run on Windows, Mac and Linux. Your courses and rounds are kept in two plain text files that you can edit yourself.

Handicap and statistics: The Handicap Index now follows the current USGA rules (the World Handicap System). Nine-hole rounds are supported, which is handy for leagues like Spark Golf. Greens In Regulation and Sand Saves are now calculated instead of entered. The Last 5 and Partial Rounds are gone, since a nine-hole round is now a round of its own.

Rounds: Type in a round with the cursor moving by itself down the scores and then the putts, with F and S keys for fairways and sand. Saved rounds can be edited or deleted. Old rounds can be kept as historical rounds, which you can count in your statistics or leave out. The Show box on the Rounds screen can pick the 18-hole rounds, the front nines or the back nines on a set of tees, and a double-click on a Stats Board row shows exactly the rounds in that row.

Courses: Courses and their sets of tees are typed in on the New Course screen, with the city and state checked against the ZIP code, and can be corrected or deleted there.

Looking at your golf: The Stats Board shows your statistics course by course and tee by tee, with your nine-hole rounds kept apart. Best Round shows your lowest round on a course, or a composite of your best score on every hole. The Analyzer shows how you play every hole of a course, from how often you make each score to your putts, fairways and greens, with your hardest holes in red and your easiest in gold. Print puts seven reports on paper or in a PDF, from your Handicap Report and a log of your rounds to a scorecard to carry on the course, and saves the ones that are tables as spreadsheet files.

Looking after your data: Import adds rounds and courses from another copy of the program or from a spreadsheet, after showing you what it will add. Export makes a copy of everything, one course, or your rounds from a date, ready for Import. Clear lets you start fresh. Every change to your files first keeps a copy of them in a dated folder of its own in the backups folder. Putting one back is just copying its files over yours, and Settings lets you choose how many to keep and have them copied to a USB stick or a cloud folder too.

Around the program: Every screen has its own photo. Press and hold the yellow Golf Analyst text at the bottom left of any screen to see all of it. The i and ? buttons explain every screen. Every dialog box has the same wooden look. Settings keeps your name, your backup choices and which data folder to use.
