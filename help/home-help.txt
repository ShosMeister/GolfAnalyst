Golf Analyst Home - Help

Okay, so you want to know how to use this thing. Here is how it all works, from the scorecard to the screen.

# Before you play

There really isn't much more you need to do when you play a round to capture the information. Normally, you write down your score. Now, instead of just your score, mark an "F" on the scorecard if you hit the fairway from the tee. Also, when you get to the green, count the number of putts and mark that on your scorecard as well. What I do is use two lines on the card. The top line is my normal score and the second line I use for the F and the number of putts (F2). Finally, if you are in the sand at any time on the hole, just put an "S" on that second line. Once you get the hang of it you'll find that it's really not that much work.

Two rules to remember. A putt is only counted when your ball is on the putting green. A stroke from the fringe or the collar is not a putt, even if you use your putter. And if you hole out from off the green (a chip-in, for example) that hole has 0 putts.

A tip for keeping up the pace of play. For the handicap, no hole counts for more than a net double bogey: par plus 2, plus any handicap strokes you get on that hole (par plus 5 until you have a Handicap Index). So once you reach that number, you can pick up, write it down and move on. Counting out an 8 on a hole that only counts as a 7 does nothing for your handicap, and the program applies the limit for you. That is also why the New Course screen asks for the handicap number of every hole: it needs them to know where you get strokes.

# After you play

To add the round, press New Round. Fill it in from your scorecard and press Save. You can even type the same F and S letters you marked on your card, right in the Putt box. The Help button on that screen walks you through it. When you come back to Home, your handicap and statistics already include the new round.

To look back at your rounds, press Rounds. It opens at your most recent round and the arrows at the bottom step through the others. You can limit it to one course, or one set of tees, with the Show box.

To fix a round you have saved, press Edit Round; the ? button on that screen walks you through it.

To change the name, click it in the Golfer's Stats section, type the name and press OK.

# Adding a course

You cannot enter a round until its course is in the program. Press New Course, type the course name and zip, then each set of tees from the scorecard, and press Save. The ? button on that screen walks you through it.

# Your own data

When you first open the program it shows the sample data, so there is something to look at. The title bar and the bar along the bottom say so. The first time you save a course or a round, the program asks whether to make your own data folder. Say Yes, then close the program and open it again: from then on it uses your own data folder and the sample data is left alone.

# Looking course by course

Press Stats Board to see your statistics for each course and set of tees, side by side. Click a heading to sort by it, and look at the gold: it marks the best figure in each column. Double-click a course to see the rounds you played there. The ? button on that screen tells you more.

# Your best golf

Press Best Round to see your lowest round on a course and set of tees. Click Composite there to see your best score on every hole put together into one round: what you could shoot if every hole went as well as it ever has. The ? button on that screen tells you more.

# Using what the numbers tell you

Look at your four percentages: Fairways Hit, Greens In Reg, Sand Saves and Two-Putt. See which one is low compared with the others. That is where to practice.

For example, I know, from using this program, that my weakness is my short game. How? Simple, my Two-Putt percentage is always 90+ but my Greens In Reg is always under 20%. That tells me that if I could get to the green in regulation, I'd be making par almost all of the time.

# Old rounds

Rounds too old to count toward today's handicap can still be kept, so nothing is lost. When you enter or edit a round, tick the Historical box. The round is saved and you can still step through it with Rounds, but it is left out of your Handicap Index. Whether it counts in your statistics is up to you: the Include historical rounds box under Golfer's Stats on this screen turns that on and off.

To see how you play today, untick Include historical rounds. The numbers on this screen, the Stats Board and Best Round then use only your recent rounds. Tick it again to bring the old ones back. Your Handicap Index never uses old rounds either way.

# Seeing the whole picture

Every screen has its own photo behind it. To see all of it, press and hold the mouse button on the yellow Golf Analyst text at the bottom left. The boxes and buttons disappear until you let go, and nothing you type while holding it goes anywhere. The pointer turns into an open hand over it, and a closed hand while you hold the button.

# Closing the program

Close the window from any screen whenever you like. Everything is written to your files the moment you press Save, so there is nothing to tidy up first. The only thing the program checks is whether you are in the middle of typing a round or a course that you have not saved yet. If you are, it asks "Discard your changes?" first. Say No to go back and finish.

# If something looks wrong

The bar along the bottom of the window tells you when something in your data files could not be read. Click it to see exactly which line of which file to fix. The program reads the files when it starts, so after you edit one by hand, close the program and open it again.
