Golf Analyst Home - Help

Okay, so you want to know how to use this thing. Here is how it all works, from the scorecard to the screen.

# Before you play

There really isn't much more you need to do when you play a round to capture the information. Normally, you write down your score. Now, instead of just your score, mark an "F" on the scorecard if you hit the fairway from the tee. Also, when you get to the green, count the number of putts and mark that on your scorecard as well. What I do is use two lines on the card. The top line is my normal score and the second line I use for the F and the number of putts (F2). Finally, if you are in the sand at any time on the hole, just put an "S" on that second line. Once you get the hang of it you'll find that it's really not that much work.

For what counts as a putt, see the Putt entry in New Round Info. The short version: a stroke from the fringe or collar is not a putt, even if you use your putter, and a chip-in has 0 putts.

A tip for keeping up the pace of play: for handicap purposes, the program caps each hole at net double bogey: par plus 2, plus any handicap strokes you get on that hole. See Home Info for the details. Once you reach that number, you can pick up, write it down and move on. The New Course screen needs the handicap number for every hole so it knows where you get strokes.

# After you play

To add the round, press New Round. Fill it in from your scorecard and press Save. You can even type the same F and S letters you marked on your card, right in the Putt box. The ? button on that screen walks you through it. When you come back to Home, your handicap and statistics already include the new round.

To look back at your rounds, press Rounds. It opens at your most recent round and the arrows at the bottom step through the others. You can limit it to one course, or one set of tees, with the Show box.

To fix a round you have saved, press Edit Round; the ? button on that screen walks you through it.

To change the name, click it in the Golfer's Stats section, type the name and press OK. You can also change it in Settings.

# Adding a course

You cannot enter a round until its course is in the program. Press New Course, type the course name and ZIP, then each set of tees from the scorecard, and press Save. The ? button on that screen walks you through it, including how to fix, correct or delete a course you have already saved.

# Your own data

When you first open the program it shows sample data, so there is something to look at. The title bar and the bar along the bottom say so. The first time you save a course or round, the program asks whether to make your own data folder. Choose Yes, then close and reopen the program. From then on it uses your own data folder and leaves the sample data alone.

# Looking course by course

Press Stats Board to see your statistics for each course and set of tees, side by side. Click a heading to sort by it, and look at the gold: it marks the best figure in each column. Double-click a course to see the rounds you played there. The ? button on that screen tells you more.

# Your best golf

Press Best Round to see your lowest round on a course and set of tees. Click Composite there to see your best score on every hole put together into one round: what you could shoot if every hole went as well as it ever has. The ? button on that screen tells you more.

# Using what the numbers tell you

Look at your four percentages: Fairways Hit, Greens In Reg, Sand Saves and Two-Putt. See which one is low compared with the others. That is where to practice.

For example, I know, from using this program, that my weakness is getting to the green. How? Simple: my Two-Putt percentage is always 90+ but my Greens In Reg is always under 20%. Once I am on the green I putt well, so if I could get there in regulation, I'd be making par almost all of the time.

# Historical rounds

When you enter or edit a historical round, check the Historical box. To include or leave out historical rounds in the Home screen statistics, the Stats Board and Best Round, check or uncheck Include historical rounds under Golfer's Stats. Home Info explains the effect on your Handicap Index and statistics.

# Seeing the whole picture

Every screen has its own photo behind it. To see all of it, press and hold the mouse button on the yellow Golf Analyst text at the bottom left. The boxes and buttons disappear until you let go, and nothing you type while holding it goes anywhere.

# Importing rounds and courses

You can add rounds and courses from another copy of Golf Analyst, or from a spreadsheet. Press Import on the Home screen.

What to pick: A courses file, a rounds file, or both. They are tab-separated text laid out like courses.tsv and rounds.tsv. To bring in another copy's data, press Choose a whole data folder... and pick its data folder; both files are filled in for you. Either file can be left empty.

From Excel: Put the column headings in the first row, then use File, Save As and choose Text (Tab delimited). Rounds need RoundID, TeeID, Date, Scores, Putts, Fairways and Sand (Holes, PCC and Historical can be left out). Courses need TeeID, Course, Tee, Rating, Slope, Pars and StrokeIndex (City, State, Zip, Holes, Yardage and the front and back nine ratings can be left out). The numbers for each hole go in one cell, separated by commas.

Check files: Press it to see what would happen. The box underneath says how many tees and rounds will be added, and lists every row that is skipped or rejected with its line number and the reason. Nothing is changed yet. A set of tees or a round you already have is skipped, and a row the program cannot read is rejected. A rounds file on its own must use the TeeIDs of your own tees.

Mark the imported rounds as historical: Check it for historical rounds you want to keep for looking back at. They never count toward your Handicap Index.

Import: It is enabled only after a check finds something to add. Before anything is written, a copy of courses.tsv and rounds.tsv goes into a folder of its own inside the backups folder, named with the date, the time and the word import (for example backups\2026-11-01_142530_import). Then the new tees and rounds are added at the end of your files; nothing you already have is changed.

Putting it back: Close the program, copy the files from that folder back over the ones in your data folder, and open the program again.

# Exporting rounds and courses

Export makes a copy of your courses and rounds that another copy of Golf Analyst can bring in with Import. Press Export on the Home screen.

What to export: Pick Everything, One course and its rounds (then pick the course from the list), or Rounds from a date. For a date, type the From date as on the round screen, for example 2026-10-22 or 10/22/2026, and leave To empty to mean today. Both days are included, and the courses and tees those rounds were played on come along too.

Include historical rounds: It starts the same as the box on the Home screen. Uncheck it to leave your historical rounds out.

Save into: Press Browse... and pick a folder. The program makes a new folder inside it named GolfAnalyst-export with the date and time, so nothing that is already there is ever written over. The line above the buttons always says how many sets of tees and rounds will be written. Then press Export; the bar along the bottom says where the copy went.

Sharing a course with a friend: Pick One course and its rounds, choose the course, and save it on a USB stick or in a cloud folder you both use. Your friend presses Import, then Choose a whole data folder..., and picks your export folder. To add only the course and not your rounds, they empty the Rounds file box before pressing Check files. Tees they already have are skipped.

Export only reads your files. Nothing in your data folder changes, so no backup is needed, and it works on the sample data too.

# Starting fresh

Clear empties your data so you can start again, for example to hand the program to another golfer. Press Clear on the Home screen and pick one of the two choices:

Clear my rounds only: Your rounds go; your courses and your name stay. Handy when the courses are right but you want to start your scoring over.

Clear everything: Your rounds, your courses, your name and your settings all go, like a brand-new copy of the program.

Then press Continue. The program asks twice before it clears anything. First it tells you exactly what will be removed; press Keep my data to stop there. Then it asks you to type CLEAR, and its OK button is enabled only once the word is typed.

A copy first: Before anything is cleared, courses.tsv, rounds.tsv and settings.txt are copied into a folder of their own inside the backups folder, named with the date, the time and the word clear (for example backups\2026-10-31_142530_clear). The program never throws away a Clear, Import or Delete course backup; Backups to keep in Settings applies only to the everyday ones.

Putting it back: Close the program, copy the files from that folder back over the ones in your data folder, and open the program again.

On the sample data Clear never changes anything. It offers to make your own data folder instead.

# Settings and your backups

Press Settings on the Home screen. Nothing changes until you press OK, and Cancel leaves everything as it was.

Backups to keep: Pick how many backups of each everyday kind to keep. 20 is plenty for most golfers; pick All if you never want any thrown away.

A copy on a USB stick or in the cloud: Press Browse... beside Also copy every backup to, and pick a folder on a USB stick or in a cloud folder such as OneDrive, Dropbox or Google Drive. From then on every backup is copied there as well, so you still have it if something happens to your computer. Press Clear to stop. If the USB stick is not plugged in, your change is still saved; the bar along the bottom just says the extra copy was not made.

Getting a backup back: Press Open backups folder. Each backup is a folder named with the date, the time and what was done, for example 2026-11-01_143012_edit-round. Close the program, copy the files from that folder back over the ones in your data folder, and open the program again. Anything you saved after that backup has to be entered again.

Moving your data: Press Change... beside Data folder and pick a folder; the program tells you what is in it. Copy my data there and use it copies your files into it (only offered when it has no data files yet); Use this folder as it is uses the files already there. Press OK. The program uses the new folder from the next time it is opened, and your old folder is left as it was.

On the sample data only your name is kept. Press Create my own data folder first.

# Closing the program

Close the window from any screen whenever you like. Everything is written to your files the moment you press Save, so there is nothing to tidy up first. The only thing the program checks is whether you are in the middle of typing a round or a course that you have not saved yet. If you are, it asks "Discard your changes?" first. Say No to go back and finish.

# If something looks wrong

The bar along the bottom of the window tells you when something in your data files could not be read. Click it to see exactly which line of which file to fix. The program reads the files when it starts, so after you edit one by hand, close the program and open it again.
